The index

DAX level

Daily close in index points with its 200-day average. Trading above a rising 200-day average is the usual definition of an uptrend.

Drawdown

Distance below the highest close so far in the window.

Realised volatility

Standard deviation of the last 21 daily returns, annualised, against its one-year average.

Inside the index

Breadth

Share of members trading above their own 50-day and 200-day averages. A rising index with falling breadth means a few large names carry it.

Average correlation

Mean of all 780 pairwise correlations of daily returns over three months. High values mean macro news drives everything; diversification inside the index then works less.

Dispersion

Spread of members' one-month returns (cross-sectional standard deviation). High dispersion means stock picking matters more than the index direction.

Sectors

Members grouped by sector and weighted by index weight. Volatility and the average correlation inside the sector use the last year of daily returns.

Data and method

  • Prices: daily closes of the 40 DAX members on Xetra in euros and the DAX index (^GDAXI), adjusted for dividends and splits, from Yahoo Finance through the open-source yfinance library, with Stooq as a fallback. End of day only, not intraday.
  • Rates and currency: ECB Data Portal API: deposit facility rate (FM), 10-year AAA yield (YC), EUR/USD reference rate (EXR).
  • Weights: . The official index uses free-float market caps, so weights are close but not exact.
  • Live updates: during Xetra hours a GitHub Actions job (live-dax40.yml) fetches 5-minute bars every 5 minutes and publishes them to the live-data branch. This page checks for a new file every 30 seconds and recomputes everything on each new bar. Yahoo delays Xetra quotes by about 15 minutes and GitHub can start scheduled jobs a few minutes late, so live figures trail the market by roughly 15–25 minutes. Real-time Xetra prices need a paid data licence.
  • Daily update: a second job runs python/build_dax40.py on weekdays at 18:20 UTC and commits the official closes to data/dax40.js. If a fetch fails, the last good data stays online.
  • Short-interval correlation: 5-minute returns understate correlation because stocks do not all trade in the same seconds (the Epps effect); the 30-minute and daily windows correct for most of it.
  • Metrics: returns use closes 1, 5, 21 and 252 sessions back. VaR and ES are historical, from the last 252 daily DAX returns. Average correlation uses the identity Σρij = Var(Σzi) for standardised returns. A new 52-week high means the highest close of the last 10 sessions beats the previous 242.
  • Members follow the index list of 30 Oct 2026; the list in the script needs updating after each quarterly review.

Data: Yahoo Finance (via yfinance), Stooq and the ECB Data Portal. Intraday prices are 5-minute bars delayed about 15 minutes by the source; nothing on this page is real-time. Index weights are approximations from market capitalisation.
